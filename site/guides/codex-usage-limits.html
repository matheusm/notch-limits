How to check Codex usage limits on a ChatGPT plan

When you sign in to Codex with ChatGPT, your plan sets how much you can use it. This guide explains how those limits work, where to check what's left, on the web and in the Codex CLI, and what to do when you reach one.

Updated 2 November 2026

How Codex limits work

Codex is included in the ChatGPT Free, Go, Plus, Pro, Business, Edu and Enterprise plans. How much of it you can use depends on the plan:

  • A five-hour window on some plans. OpenAI publishes estimates of local messages per five-hour period for Plus and Standard Business. Pro plans currently have no five-hour limit.
  • Weekly limits may also apply.
  • One allowance for local and cloud work. Local messages and cloud chats share your plan's usage allowance. ChatGPT Work also shares usage with Codex: inside ChatGPT it uses the same usage limits.
  • Flexible pricing. Enterprise and Edu users with flexible pricing have no fixed rate limits; usage scales with credits.

This guide doesn't copy the per-plan estimates. The Codex pricing page has the current ones.

Check on the web

Open the Codex usage dashboard while signed in to ChatGPT. It shows your current limits.

Check in the Codex CLI

  • /status shows your remaining usage limits during a session, such as the five-hour and weekly windows, along with the session's configuration and token usage.
  • /statusline keeps them on screen. Turn on the rate-limits item in the picker and the footer updates right away; the choice is saved to tui.status_line in config.toml.
  • /usage shows your ChatGPT token activity, daily, weekly or cumulative. When you have an earned rate-limit reset, it's also where you redeem it.
  • codex login status, run in your shell, prints the active authentication method, so you can confirm you're signed in with ChatGPT rather than an API key.

When you reach a limit

  • A turn already running can continue. If you reach your limit during an active turn, the agent can keep working on that turn, subject to fair use limits.
  • Buy credits. Plus and Pro users can buy additional credits to keep working without upgrading their plan. Business, Edu and Enterprise plans with flexible pricing can buy additional workspace credits.
  • Use an API key for local chats. Anyone can run extra local chats with an API key, charged at standard API rates.
  • Or wait for the reset. The usage dashboard and /status show where each limit stands.

Keep Codex limits in view with notch-limits

notch-limits is a small Mac app that keeps your Codex limits around the MacBook notch, next to your Claude Code accounts.

  • No request for most updates. Codex writes its rate limits to the session logs in $CODEX_HOME/sessions on every response. notch-limits reads the latest ones and only calls the usage endpoint when that data is more than 10 minutes old. Opening the panel never makes a request.
  • Every window Codex reports. Hover the notch to see each one with its percentage and reset time. Rings stay gray, turn orange from 70% and red from 90%.
  • Found on its own. ~/.codex, and folders such as ~/.codex-team, show up as accounts when they contain an auth.json, at launch and whenever you open Settings. A Codex home kept anywhere else goes in accounts.json.
  • Read-only. It reads the login in auth.json and never refreshes, rewrites or uploads tokens anywhere other than the official usage endpoint.

Good to know

  • Codex can keep its login in your operating system's credential store instead of auth.json. The cli_auth_credentials_store setting decides: file writes auth.json under CODEX_HOME, keyring uses the credential store, and auto uses the credential store when it's available and falls back to auth.json otherwise.
  • notch-limits reads only auth.json, so it doesn't find a Codex login kept in the credential store. To check, look for auth.json in your Codex home (~/.codex by default).
